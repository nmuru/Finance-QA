5. Data Limitations & Notes

  • Source granularity: The get_financial_statements tool provides normalized annual rows; subsidiary‑level or quarterly detail is not available here.
  • XBRL vs. narrative: Certain concepts (e.g., allowance for credit losses, deferred‑tax liability assets) have XBRL observations; row‑level values may be summaries. Full XBRL verification would require the structured‑JSON tools.
  • Foreign‑currency translation: Balance‑sheet and income‑statement totals include FX effects; the row data does not decompose these effects.
  • No cash‑flow‑from‑operations aggregate: The cash‑flow statement reports “Profit or Loss” and individual adjustments, but the net operating‑cash‑flow figure is not a single row; it must be assembled from the individual line items.
  • No segment breakdown: Revenue and expense segmentation (geography, line of business) is unavailable in the provided evidence.

6. Conclusion

The five‑year financial record for Infosys Ltd shows a mature IT‑services profile: stable‑ish revenue growth, moderate gross‑margin compression, and employee‑benefit costs dominating the expense structure. Key signals include:

  • Goodwill increase of 35 % in FY2025 – the most notable quantitative movement; requires disclosure review to determine whether it reflects an acquisition, business combination, or translation.
  • Progressive margin compression (gross, operating, net) largely driven by cost growth outpacing revenue, a common pattern in large services firms investing in talent and delivery capacity.
  • Leverage rising modestly but remaining at levels consistent with an investment‑grade balance sheet.
  • Cash‑profit alignment is strong, indicating limited accrual distortion.

No outright accounting irregularities are evidenced by the row‑level data alone; however, the goodwill jump and margin trend warrant review of the firm’s footnotes and MD&A for complete context. All conclusions are bounded to the verified SEC‑reported rows and the ratios derivable therefrom.
