1. Executive Summary

Infosys' top line remains healthy but has clearly matured: revenue reached $19,277M in FY2025, a +20.3% surge in FY2022 followed by sharp deceleration (+11.6% → +1.9%) and a modest re-acceleration to +3.8% in FY2025 (3-yr CAGR FY2022–FY2025: +2.3%, below the double-digit threshold of a "high growth" engine). Profitability, however, is under sustained pressure: gross margin compressed ~440 bps, operating margin ~340 bps, and net margin ~290 bps over the five-year window, driven primarily by the employee-benefits/cost-of-revenue line. Earnings quality is sound — EPS growth has been buttressed by buybacks concentrated in FY2022–FY2023 (~$2.9B of share purchases), but over the most recent two years EPS and absolute net income have moved essentially in lockstep, indicating a return to organic earnings expansion.

Supporting verified components:

  • Gross profit: $4,733M → $5,872M (FY2021→FY2025) but gross margin eroded from 34.9% to 30.5%.
  • Cost of revenue (incl. employee benefits) rose $8,828M → $13,405M; employee benefits expense alone grew from $7,493M to $10,165M (now ~52.7% of revenue), the principal driver of core cost pressure.
  • Operating profit: $3,325M → $4,071M; net profit attributable to owners: $2,613M → $3,158M.

Analysis:

  • The company is losing operating leverage: both gross and operating margins are ~300–440 bps below FY2021 levels, and FY2025 marks a partial recovery only.
  • However, operating margin compression (−341 bps) is narrower than gross margin compression (−441 bps), indicating partial offset from cost discipline in the non-core/operating expense base (R&D expense up only modestly; depreciation, finance costs, and professional fees largely contained). This is consistent with the pattern: SG&A/overhead efficiency partially offsetting core cost (employee) pressures.
  • Net margin troughs occurred in FY2023/FY2025 (16.4%); the FY2024 improvement to 17.1% was aided by a one-year deferred-tax reversal (+$162M), which detracted again in FY2025 (−$150M). Hence the most recent net-margin readings are partly tax-timing, not purely operational, signals.

  • Recent two years (FY2023→FY2025): EPS +7.0% vs. net income +5.9% — essentially aligned (Δ ~110 bps), i.e., not a >300 bps divergence. EPS growth is no longer being substantially subsidized by share repurchases in this window; it is tracking organic results.
  • Internal consistency check (verified): the implied EPS (net income ÷ diluted shares) equals the reported diluted EPS (e.g., FY2025: $3,158M ÷ 4,144M = $0.76; FY2024: $3,167M ÷ 4,140M = $0.76). No sign of a negative-net-income/positive-EPS anomaly; reported EPS values are rounded to cents.
  • Earnings-quality caveats (verified via cash-flow statement): reported profit is subject to notable non-operating swings — exchange-rate adjustments, share-based payment expenses, and tax-timing effects driven by a current-tax expense that rose from $1,015M (FY2024) to $1,435M (FY2025) and a deferred-tax reversal of −$150M in FY2025, even as the applicable tax rate dropped from 34.94% to 25.17%. Net income quality remains positive but is not purely operating in composition.

5. Data Limitations & Source Notes

  • EPS values are reported to two decimals in the underlying filing; small rounding is present (computed EPS vs. reported EPS agree within $0.01).
  • Company-context remarks (e.g., demand normalization) are labeled as analytical inference where stated; all figures above are from the retrieved SEC evidence.
