Executive Summary

IBM's capital deployment strategy over the past five years has been defined by three pillars: sustained and growing dividend distributions, aggressive share repurchases of talent via equity compensation, and a dramatic escalation in M&A activity in FY 2025. Operating cash flow has remained remarkably stable in the $10.4B–$13.9B range, but FY 2025's $8.3B acquisition spend — nearly triple the FY 2024 level — pushed total capital deployment above internal cash generation, forcing a material increase in long-term debt and a drawdown of cash reserves. The strategy appears to be a high-conviction bet on inorganic growth, funded by a strong balance sheet.

6. Key Takeaways

1. IBM is running a high-conviction M&A strategy in FY 2025, deploying $8.3B on acquisitions — the largest annual outlay in five years — funded by incremental debt and cash reserves rather than internally generated cash alone.

2. The dividend is well-covered at ~1.94x FCF, and management has treated it as a priority that must be maintained regardless of other capital allocation decisions.

3. Free cash flow generation is robust but plateauing. FCF has been in the $12–13B range since FY 2023, with the FY 2025 dip partly attributable to working capital dynamics. The light capex model (1.6% of revenue) is a structural advantage.

4. The balance sheet has absorbed the FY 2025 spending. Net debt jumped $5.3B to $41.25B, and goodwill reached $67.72B (44.6% of total assets). The equity base ($32.65B) provides a meaningful but not generous cushion.

5. Earnings quality improved dramatically in FY 2025. Net income of $10.59B (vs. $6.02B in FY 2024) with a negative effective tax rate (tax benefit of -$242M) and strong operating income suggest the deployment strategy may be bearing fruit, though the sustainability of the tax benefit merits attention.
